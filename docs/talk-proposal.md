# IASEAI 2027 Talk Proposal

Prepared 6 October 2026. Submission status: not confirmed.

## Title

Communicating AI Risk Across Languages: Video Narratives and Audience Responses on Bilibili and YouTube

## Proposal text

Communicating frontier AI risk internationally requires understanding the information environments through which audiences encounter it. Translating a warning does not necessarily preserve its meaning: a video title, a long-form explanation, and the discussion beneath it each offer a different view of the same event.

This talk introduces an ongoing comparative project examining coverage of three AI safety-related events across English, Simplified Chinese, and Traditional Chinese video content on Bilibili and YouTube. Its corpus includes 4,331 event-related titles, a selected set of 304 video transcripts, and 5,967 comments and timestamped on-screen messages linked to those selected videos. The selection targets cumulative view coverage within event and content strata; it is not a representative sample of the public.

The presentation will explain how we distinguish attention-seeking titles from substantive narratives, examine societal threat and efficacy framing, and read audience responses in context. It will use illustrative examples to explore how humor, blame, skepticism, and requests for actionable responses complicate the interpretation of apparent agreement or dismissal. These examples will be treated as situated discourse, rather than direct measurements of private attitudes.

The practical contribution is a set of questions for AI safety educators and communicators: What risk is being described? Who is portrayed as responsible? What response is available to the audience? How can communication invite informed participation across different language and platform contexts?

The study is undergoing recoding and validation. This proposal does not claim established differences between ecosystems or a causal effect of video framing on audience behavior. The talk will clearly distinguish verified observations, illustrative cases, and communication proposals, with any quantitative findings included only after validation. Attendees will leave with a concrete approach to auditing AI risk communication across languages and a clearer sense of the evidence needed to evaluate it.

## Short abstract (alternative for a shorter portal field)

How do audiences encounter frontier AI risk across language and platform contexts? This talk introduces an ongoing comparative project on three AI safety-related events in English, Simplified Chinese, and Traditional Chinese video content on Bilibili and YouTube. The corpus comprises 4,331 titles, 304 selected video transcripts, and 5,967 linked audience interactions. We distinguish attention-seeking titles from substantive narratives and examine threat, efficacy, and audience attribution in context. Illustrative cases show why humor, skepticism, and blame should not be read as transparent measures of private attitudes. The presentation offers practical questions for auditing risk communication: what is threatened, who is responsible, and what action is available? Recoding and validation are ongoing; the talk will distinguish verified observations from illustrative cases and proposals, without claiming causal effects or population representativeness.

## Relevance and intended audience

Relevant to Information integrity & persuasion; Economy, work, & education; and International governance. Intended for AI safety educators, science communicators, community organizers, and researchers or policymakers seeking to communicate risk across languages.

## Audience takeaways

- Separate the claims made in a title from the explanation developed in a video.
- Interpret humor and attribution in audience discourse without equating them with private beliefs.
- Audit whether risk communication identifies responsibility and offers meaningful routes to informed participation.
