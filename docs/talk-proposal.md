# IASEAI 2027 Talk Proposal

Prepared 6 October 2026. Submission status: not confirmed.

## Title

Communicating AI Risk Across Languages: Video Narratives and Audience Responses on Bilibili and YouTube

## Proposal text

Communicating frontier AI risk internationally requires understanding the information environments through which audiences encounter it. This talk presents preliminary descriptive findings from a comparative study of English, Simplified Chinese, and Traditional Chinese video content on Bilibili and YouTube.

The event-title pool contains 4,331 titles across three AI safety-related events. The selected transcript set contains 304 videos, with 278 in the core comparison and 26 in an exploratory extension. Reception analysis uses 5,967 linked interactions: 4,649 comments and 1,318 timestamped on-screen messages. Selection targets cumulative view coverage within event and content strata; it does not represent the public.

Three observations structure the talk. First, risk emphasis often coexists with institutional recommendations: these appear in 13 of 21 risk-more Simplified Chinese videos, 10 of 10 Traditional Chinese videos, and 165 of 201 English videos. Second, recommendations mostly address industry or political institutions, leaving the relationship between institutional action and audience participation open. Third, technical or neutral discourse is the largest comment category in all three groups, while cynical motive attribution, defensive othering, and unclassifiable responses vary across the selected content groups, events, and interaction channels.

These patterns complicate a simple account in which stronger risk language necessarily closes constructive response. They do not establish a causal effect, a population difference, or an efficacy mechanism. Full-transcript risk-benefit coding can include off-topic discourse; benefit is not response efficacy, and comments are observable expression rather than private attitudes.

Recoding and descriptive aggregation are complete. A public repository provides reduced coded data, coding materials, and executable aggregation checks. Independent semantic review, reliability, sensitivity analysis, and confounder adjustment remain outstanding. Attendees will learn how to audit what is threatened, who is responsible, and what action is available, while separating observations from communication proposals.

## Short abstract (alternative for a shorter portal field)

How do audiences encounter frontier AI risk across language and platform contexts? This talk introduces an ongoing comparative project on three AI safety-related events in English, Simplified Chinese, and Traditional Chinese video content on Bilibili and YouTube. The corpus comprises 4,331 titles, 304 selected video transcripts, and 5,967 linked audience interactions. We distinguish attention-seeking titles from substantive narratives and examine risk emphasis, institutional recommendations, and audience attribution in context. Illustrative cases show why humor, skepticism, and blame should not be read as transparent measures of private attitudes. The presentation offers practical questions for auditing risk communication: what is threatened, who is responsible, and what action is available? Recoding and descriptive aggregation are complete; independent validation is ongoing. the talk will distinguish verified observations from illustrative cases and proposals, without claiming causal effects or population representativeness.

## Relevance and intended audience

Relevant to Information integrity & persuasion; Economy, work, & education; and International governance. Intended for AI safety educators, science communicators, community organizers, and researchers or policymakers seeking to communicate risk across languages.

## Audience takeaways

- Separate the claims made in a title from the explanation developed in a video.
- Interpret humor and attribution in audience discourse without equating them with private beliefs.
- Audit whether risk communication identifies responsibility and offers meaningful routes to informed participation.
